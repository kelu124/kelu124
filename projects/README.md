## Catalogue

### Ultrasound hardware

_Boards and devkits for pulse-echo ultrasound._

- **[echomods](https://github.com/kelu124/echomods)** ⭐ 460 · 🍴 123 · NOASSERTION `raspberry-pi` `beaglebone` `ice40` `hx4k` `up5k` `arduino` `altium` `kicad` `fpga`  
  The historical mothership: a low-cost, open-source kit of modules (pulser, analog front end, ADC, motherboard) to let scientists, hackers and makers hack their way to ultrasound imaging for under 500$ at home. Years of raw experiment logs, probes, notebooks and the article in the Journal of Open Hardware all live here. Not a medical scanner, but a great pedagogical and NDT playground, and the ancestor of un0rick and pic0rick.  
  <sub>ultrasound imaging, open source hardware, modules, pulser, analog front end, experiments log, journal of open hardware, ndt</sub>
- **[murgen-dev-kit](https://github.com/kelu124/murgen-dev-kit)** ⭐ 308 · 🍴 75 · NOASSERTION `arduino` `altium` `beaglebone`  
  The 2016 Murgen open-source ultrasound imaging dev-kit: get a home-made single-element pulse-echo machine running in a garage, hackerspace or fablab for under 500$, with a BitScope, an Arduino/Trinket and a hacked transducer. Where the first (rough!) echo images came from, now superseded by echomods and un0rick.  
  <sub>ultrasound imaging, dev kit, pulse echo, bitscope, first image, echopen, hackaday</sub>
- **[un0rick](https://github.com/kelu124/un0rick)** ⭐ 177 · 🍴 31 · NOASSERTION `ice40` `hx4k` `fpga` `raspberry-pi` `ftdi`  
  A smallish single-channel ultrasound pulse-echo board around a Lattice iCE40HX4K: 64 Msps ADC, AD8331 time-gain compensation, MD1210 pulser and 8 Mbit SRAM, fully controlled over SPI from a Raspberry Pi, USB (FTDI) or even an M5Stack. Open toolchain for the gateware, python lib and tutorials included. OSHWA certified FR000005, and the board that made un0rick.cc a thing.  
  <sub>ultrasound, pulse echo, single channel, 64msps, ad8331, tgc, spi, oshwa certified, tapr</sub>
- **[pic0rick](https://github.com/kelu124/pic0rick)** ⭐ 113 · 🍴 28 · NOASSERTION `rp2040` `rp2350` `max14866` `kicad`  
  The current flagship: an RP2040 (or RP2350) based acquisition board for pulse-echo ultrasound, with a 60 Msps 10-bit ADC, protected front end and AD8331 + MCP4812 time-gain compensation. A single PMOD takes the pulser + HV board (three-level pulses via MD1210 + TC6320), a double PMOD takes a VGA output, a MAX14866 mux or whatever you fancy. Both PIOs do the heavy lifting. KiCad files, unified RP2040/RP2350 firmware and notebooks are published, and boards are on Tindie.  
  <sub>ultrasound, pulse echo, acquisition board, 60msps, pio, tgc, pmod, pulser, oshwa certified, tindie</sub>
- **[lit3rick](https://github.com/kelu124/lit3rick)** ⭐ 46 · 🍴 21 `up5k` `ice40` `fpga` `raspberry-pi`  
  An up5k board to manage pulse-echo ultrasound acquisition: Lattice iCE40 UP5K, AD9629 12-bit ADC at 64 Msps, AD8331 gain, HV7361 pulser up to +-100V and a MCP4812 DAC for 8 us gain segments. Programmed with no FTDI at all, straight through the Raspberry Pi header, and the gateware can compute an FFT on board and stream it over I2S. OSHWA certified FR000006.  
  <sub>ultrasound, pulse echo, ad9629, fft on fpga, i2s, spi, oshwa certified, tapr, verilog</sub>
- **[Analog_Frontend](https://github.com/kelu124/Analog_Frontend)** ⭐ 4 · 🍴 3 `kicad`  
  KiCad design files for the analog circuitry of one channel of an ultrasound imaging system (transmitter + receiver), with an AD8331-based VGA subcircuit and several transmitter variants (single transistor, push-pull MOSFETs, MD1213 + TC6320). Designed by William Meng, Cindy Xiao and Ruoyu Sheng; mirrored here.  
  <sub>analog front end, transmitter, receiver, ad8331, md1213, kicad, single channel</sub>
- **[pic32arick](https://github.com/kelu124/pic32arick)** ⭐ 4 `pic32`  
  Why not use a PIC32AK1216GC41064 as a pulse-echo device? It has two 40 Msps 12-bit ADCs, three 100 MHz op-amps, DACs and high-res PWM on chip. Design notes for a 5V pulser, MD0100 protection, I2C-controlled gain, a Raspberry Pi header and a SAO connector. Early stage, in the backlog.  
  <sub>pulse echo, microcontroller, 40msps, on-chip opamp, sao, backlog</sub>
- **[minus](https://github.com/kelu124/minus)** ⭐ 2 `rp2350` `kicad`  
  A minimal, single-channel, low-cost open-source pulse-echo platform designed as a conference-workshop badge: RP2350, a 3-4 MHz piezo, unipolar HV pulser, VGA/TGC and external ADC, USB-C, OLED and a Raspberry Pi header. Early design stage: requirements, survey of comparable systems and component trade study so far.  
  <sub>ultrasound, badge, workshop, minimal, a-mode, m-mode, muscle monitoring, requirements</sub>
- **[ultr4rick](https://github.com/kelu124/ultr4rick)** ⭐ 2 · 🍴 1 · NOASSERTION `rp2350` `max14866` `kicad`  
  The higher-power successor to pic0rick: an RP2350B with a 60 Msps 10-bit AD9215 ADC behind an AD8331 front end, a bipolar HV pulser fed by a SEPIC supply, a MAX14866 16-channel HV mux with 8 U.FL element connectors, uSD, RGB LED and USB-C. The firmware computes the Hilbert envelope and A-law compression of each capture on board. In the works.  
  <sub>ultrasound, pulse echo, rp2350b, bipolar pulser, hv mux, hilbert envelope, a-law, usd</sub>
- **[pic0dvi](https://github.com/kelu124/pic0dvi)** ⭐ 1 · NOASSERTION `rp2040` `kicad`  
  A pic0rick variant where the double PMOD connector is replaced by an HDMI connector, to push acquisitions out fast as DVI. Same 60 Msps front end, pulser PMOD and rp2040 firmware. TAPR licensed.  
  <sub>pic0rick, dvi, hdmi, video out, pulse echo</sub>
- **[UltrasoundBox](https://github.com/kelu124/UltrasoundBox)** ⭐ 1 · 🍴 2

  <sub>experiment, 2017</sub>

### Ultrasound software & libraries

_Drivers, signal processing and probe reverse-engineering._

- **[pyusbus](https://github.com/kelu124/pyusbus)** ⭐ 31 · 🍴 9 · GPL-3.0  
  Python APIs for cheap USB ultrasound probes: get images in three lines of code from an Interson mechanical probe, a BMV convex, a Healson linear HP20L or a BMV linear doppler. Reverse-engineered protocols, udev rules and notebooks, work in progress but already producing frames of forearms and vein phantoms.  
  <sub>usb probe, interson, healson, bmv, reverse engineering, python api, b-mode, udev</sub>
- **[us_rf_processing](https://github.com/kelu124/us_rf_processing)** ⭐ 31 · 🍴 8  
  Notebooks showing how to rebuild a B-mode ultrasound image from raw RF data: filtering, envelope, scan conversion, NDT experiments and signal compression (A-law). Comes with a set of RF datasets (npz) acquired with un0rick and various probes (hp2121, kretz, ATL3, Bard 3.5...).  
  <sub>rf processing, b-mode, scan conversion, datasets, npz, a-law compression, ndt, notebooks</sub>
- **[pyUProbe1](https://github.com/kelu124/pyUProbe1)** ⭐ 18 · 🍴 2  
  Python barebones to talk to a UProbe-1 wireless ultrasound probe: PCAP captures of the wifi traffic, listeners on the data and info ports, notebooks to understand headers, rebuild videos and even write a pseudo-valid DICOM. CC-BY-SA.  
  <sub>uprobe-1, wireless probe, reverse engineering, pcap, dicom, python</sub>
- **[pyUn0-lib](https://github.com/kelu124/pyUn0-lib)** ⭐ 5 `raspberry-pi` `ice40`  
  The un0rick companion python module (python 2 and 3) to drive the board from a Raspberry Pi over the 40-pin ribbon: TGC curves, pulse timings, sampling rate, single or multi-line acquisitions, with results saved as JSON for processing.  
  <sub>un0rick, python library, spi, tgc, acquisition, raspberry pi</sub>
- **[python-usb-un0rick](https://github.com/kelu124/python-usb-un0rick)** ⭐ 4 · 🍴 3 · GPL-3.0 `ftdi` `ice40`  
  How to set up and use the un0rick with only a USB cable: jumpers, iceprog to flash the bitstream through the on-board FTDI, udev rules, and the python lib (pyftdi based) with a test bench producing a series of images. GPL-3.0.  
  <sub>un0rick, usb, pyftdi, iceprog, setup guide, python</sub>
- **[pyS3](https://github.com/kelu124/pyS3)** ⭐ 1 · MIT  
  Notes and code to talk to an S3 wifi ultrasound probe: decompiled Java references (probe IP and port, TGC curves, frame structure) as a starting point for a python client.  
  <sub>wifi probe, reverse engineering, tgc, frame format</sub>

- [fpga_fibre_scan](https://github.com/kelu124/fpga_fibre_scan) —   本信号处理板主要由FPGA芯片和CYUSB3.0 芯片组成,其中FPGA模块主要完成与相关外设的交互，CYUSB3.0主要完成协议数据的传输。      2.2.1  FPGA模块         处理流程：   1. 链路初始化： 在上位机完成USB固件的下载，并读取固件的信息状态描述后，通过上电复位或者手动复位，通过串口发送0X55给上位机，表明链路打通，一次握手成功。 2. 超声波发射与AD数据接收：在收到上位机通过串口发送的0X02指令后，开启（START），发送超声方波信号，（注：该START信号在处理过程被改变成包络信号）因为只是单阵元，所以就没有接收延迟聚焦的问题，但有皮肤表皮的客观实际和单阵元回波的时间消耗，所以在等到C_CORDIC_DELAY（1000）后，才开始AD数据的采集。（注：具体多少厚度，需要细算）。每次采集4096个数据，形成一个扫描线；总共需要采集300根扫描线，若不够，则需重新发送方波，并接收AD数据。 3.  剪切波发送： 在采集到第33根扫描线后，开始剪切波的发送，简单的发送50HZ的单载波就可以，此后的AD数据就含有剪切波的信息。 4.  控制通路的信息：  这里通过CYUSB3.0的串口来传送上位机发送的控制端口信息 ，包括数据通路的读和写指令（注：这里只需要通过BULK读取数据通路的数据，不需要通过BULK向数据通路写数据）；通过CYUSB3.0的串口来传送下位机FPGA的状态信息指令给上位机。（由于采用的是URAT，所以有FIFO缓存和数据发送接收状态控制操作） 5. 数据通路的信息： 这里通过上位机的读写指令来将数据存储到FIFO中，这里默认发送的是0X00指令，一直读取AD采集到的数据。并且采用的是BULK的Xfer->read的同步传输，一直要等到指定数目数据（4096*300）采集完才结束采集。     2.2.2   USB3.0模块          1. 这里首先要进行存储划分和寄存器映射，一般汇编或者其他CMD格式，然后编写BOOTLOAD汇编，最后中断跳转处理（汇编）。      2. 这里主要配置GPIF的异步串口参数和读写操作。      3. 这里需要给出CTL端口和BULK端口的配置和读写。     2.3  上位机软件      这里主要完成算法的处理和界面的显示和控制。 关于算法部分需要后面补充，目前没有完全消化。       处理流程：   1. 初始化USB，然后上位机通过控制端点发送写命令控制字（不加帧头命令）下位机未处理，开启监视工作线程循环，主要内容是：通过控制端点发送读命令控制字，通过控制端点读回串口信息，用来验证设备是否启动握手成功（0X55）。 2. 启动成功后引发响应的启动触发方法。启动触发方法中，先要延时大于0.36s，如果选中check_box，则使用存储的测试数据，若未选中，通过控制端点发送写start命令，开启bulk端口读循环线程，最后每次测量发送一次读bulk数据消息到消息队列。 3. 在bulk端口读循环线程中引发响应的bulk读方法，在bulk读方法中，主要调用底层的USB3.0的bulkin读方法，数据读上来后，post一个getData消息，交由绑定的函数来处理数据。 4.  数据处理包括二独立部分，一部分是原始数据产生MotionMOdel信息 ,一部分是原始数据产生剪切波速度和杨氏模量信息。
